The Empty Space That Makes a Network

What a cholera map, a letterform, and random points reveal about geometric graphs

Heriberto Espino-Montelongo · UDLAP   Héctor Maravillo · UAM, Mexico

Interactive author preview — not published or endorsed by SIAM News. The original software and diagrams are open for inspection.

A map records where things are; it does not decide which ones should be connected. A surprising class of geometric networks answers that question by inspecting the space between points—often, the absence of a third point determines whether an edge exists.

A Map Was Never a Network

In 1854 John Snow mapped cholera deaths and water pumps in London's Soho district. He did not assign households to their closest pump through a Voronoi diagram. Our modern overlay does: thirteen pumps create nearest-site cells, while 578 death locations remain historical observations. The resulting partition is useful precisely because its limitations are visible—street access, individual water use and exposure cannot be read from Euclidean distance alone.

Gabriel and Sokal made emptiness a connection rule in their 1969 study of geographic variation: two sample sites became neighbors when their diameter disk contained no competing point. Toussaint's relative neighborhood graph (1980) replaced that disk with the intersection of two equal disks, a lune. Later β-skeletons, γ-neighborhoods and other variants changed the excluded region. Each alternative makes a different assumption about what counts as nearby.

Inspect the Decisions, Not Just the Drawings

The print-oriented draft selects three figures; this online companion keeps all four live constructions. Move one point and watch a cell move, a letter's counter close, or a candidate link disappear. Then keep the points fixed and switch graph rules: the difference comes from the mathematical model, not the sample.

The Third Point Has a Veto

An edge between p and q survives when their excluded region contains no other observed site. For a finite point set P and forbidden region K(p,q), the decision is

{p,q} ∈ EK(P) ⇔ K(p,q) ∩ (P ∖ {p,q}) = ∅(1)

Change the region and the connections change. Gabriel uses a disk; relative neighborhood uses a lune; β-skeletons adjust its geometry continuously. A rejected edge carries a geometric explanation, but a surviving edge is still only a candidate physical radio link.

How Many Neighbors Should Random Points Have?

For a homogeneous Poisson process of intensity ρ in Rd, suppose the forbidden-region volume is aKrd at separation r. The probability of an edge is exactly exp(−ρaKrd) under the two-point conditioning. Integrating over possible neighbors produces the striking identity

E[deg] = vd / aK(2)

Here vd is unit-ball volume. Intensity cancels: for scale-invariant forbidden regions, denser Poisson sites have shorter edges but the same expected degree. In two dimensions Gabriel predicts 4, while the relative neighborhood graph predicts about 2.56. Observed finite graphs need not follow these constants; boundary effects, clustered sampling and inhomogeneity matter. Under further assumptions, total edge length and edge counts also admit statistical limit theorems.

Beyond the Plane

The empty-region test applies mathematically to Euclidean Rd for any positive dimension, not just to maps. That makes the idea relevant to scientific feature vectors, manifold learning and learned representations. In particular, Dyballa and Zucker (2023) use Gabriel neighborhoods to initialize a method for estimating manifold geometry. This is a research connection, not a claim that our package contains a graph-neural-network trainer or a vector database: most current constructors and these live demonstrations remain principally planar.

A Common Laboratory for Competing Rules

The open-source ProximityGraphs Python package lets researchers generate seeded point sets, switch among Gabriel, RNG, Delaunay, β- and γ-neighborhood, nearest-neighbor and alpha constructions, and compare degree, length, components and sensitivity. SetPoints supplies the samples; Experiment supports reproducible repetitions. Original defaults come from the pinned Python implementation; edited browser scenes are computed separately in JavaScript.

One sensitivity experiment fixes the points and varies the neighbor rule; another fixes the rule and resamples the points. They answer different questions. A short link might spare construction material but lengthen routes; a spurious link could shortcut folds in a learned manifold; a noisy boundary point could close a pore. Install pip install proximitygraphs or open the full laboratory to investigate those consequences rather than simply admire a graph.
